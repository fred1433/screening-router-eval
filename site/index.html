Open-model routing for screening recommendations

The question: Can a smaller open-weight model prepare a defensible screening recommendation, and when does calling a larger model actually improve it, while preserving analyst sign-off?

Run v1 covered 80 unseen records from the same 10 authored case families, each run 3 times. Correct and supported among answerable runs: small model 176/180, large model 178/180, router 180/180. No configuration recommended excluding a true match: none of 51 true-match cases in any run, a 95% interval of 0 to 7%. Scored on five separate dimensions, the same answers still carry faults the decision score hid: next actions with authorizing language, and evidence cited from the wrong line. A stricter citation check added after the run would send back 9 of 235 small-model outputs, 3 of 238 large-model outputs and 3 of 240 router outputs.

Against the small model alone, the router won 4 runs over 2 cases and lost 0, at a resource index of 1.72 against 1 and a p95 latency of 24.9 s against 13.4 s. Against the large model alone it used a resource index of 1.72 against 6.47. Stopping the router before its large-model call gives 174/180, so the large-model step accounts for 6 runs.

Controls v2 were written after inspecting the v1 results, so on these 80 cases v2 is not a measurement on unseen data. Replayed on the recorded answers without a new model call, they leave the router at 180/180 correct and supported and send 23 runs back for a repair call that was not made. 31 new cases generated after the v2 freeze are the unseen test; so far only the rules have run on them.

Scope: the identity disposition for a list candidate already selected upstream; adverse media, risk scoring and PEP screening are out. Customer records are synthetic. Candidate records are copied field by field from the OFAC SDN export published 2026-10-02, with the entry UID kept; nothing is alleged about anyone. Models: qwen/qwen3.5-9b on parasail/bf16 and qwen/qwen3.5-397b-a17b on deepinfra/fp8, through OpenRouter with fallbacks disabled. The page as first published is kept at v1.

Case files, as the analyst would receive them

Each file holds the customer documents, the list candidate, the proposed disposition with its citations, the route, every check in order and the next action. Each dimension is scored on its own, so a right decision can sit next to evidence or a next action that still needs correcting. Nothing is approved by the system.

How to read it
Counts are runs: 51 true-match, 29 distinct-entity and 20 insufficient-evidence cases, three runs each. Runs at temperature 0 are not independent, so intervals are computed over cases. Zero observed, not zero risk. The dimension rows count failures over the runs where the dimension applies.
What the policy check intercepted
15 wrong exclusion recommendations in the small model's first answers, from 5 cases, all in the contradictory identifiers family; 0 in the large model's. None reached a final answer.
Two ways to count the router
Inside the router, the large-model step corrected 6 runs (2 cases) of the router's own intermediate small output, and replaced 4 failed small outputs with valid stops. Against the small model alone, an independent execution, the router won 4 runs. The numbers differ because the router's small output and the small-alone run are separate calls.
Escalations
34 of 240 router runs: contradictory identifiers (15); match rests on a name spelling that is not listed (12); small output failed checks after repair (7). Rules alone decided 93 runs; 11 stopped for missing evidence without a large-model call.
Transport
7 of 783 model calls needed a transport retry. No case remained unresolved after transport retries. What the failed attempts consumed is unknown: v1 did not record them; v2 records every attempt.

Two policies
One individual whose customer record has its passport number removed. Policy A (two agreeing identifiers, at least one strong) gives same entity, supported from the small model and same entity, supported from the rules; policy B (two agreeing strong identifiers) gives insufficient evidence and insufficient evidence. The policy is an input, not something the model decides. Which kinds of identifier mismatch may exclude a match is a decision the institution approves; the illustrative policy v2 no longer lets a passport mismatch alone exclude.
Invented names
Every name token replaced by an invented one on 43 true-match cases. Over the answerable ones, the small model went from 26/26 to 24/26, the large model from 26/26 to 26/26, the router from 26/26 to 24/26. The 2 changed cases each claim a place-of-birth agreement the file does not support. This shows sensitivity to the names, not whether prior knowledge of listed names helped.

What v2 changes, and the new cases

Controls v2, written after reading v1

  • One test for an absent value on both sides, so not listed never conflicts; the rules' output now crosses the same validator as the models'.
  • For a supported match the next action is fixed text: review and refer for the sanctions decision; it never authorizes onboarding. Authorizing language is rejected everywhere else.
  • Code computes exact and listed alias; a model claim the code does not confirm sends the router to the large model, and a name that normalises to nothing stops for a person.
  • Evidence is bound to its field and to the customer: a Residence country line does not prove a nationality, a sentence about a brother proves nothing about the customer.
  • 30 of 30 tests pass, including one showing the four configurations cross the same validator.

31 new cases, generated after the v2 freeze

Families: a customer identifier the list does not give (6), a renewed passport (6), alternative dates of birth (5), two customer documents that disagree (5), a company homonym in the same country and city (5), long names (4). References follow policy v2; a fresh model session reviewed them twice (1 disagreement in round 1, fixed in the generator; 0 in round 2). Rules only, one run: 17 correct and supported, 11 right stops, 3 needless stops, 0 incorrect, 0/26 wrong exclusions. The model runs on these cases are not done yet: this demo spends nothing on model APIs, so they wait for an agreed run.

Reproduce the results

./recompute.sh rebuilds every number on this page from the recorded traces, without network or key. ./run_final.sh runs a new experiment and needs OPENROUTER_API_KEY.

What this does not show

  • Performance on real customer files. The customers are synthetic and the case families were authored by the builder.
  • Expert agreement. References were checked by a fresh model session, not by a compliance analyst.
  • v2 on unseen data with models. On the 80 v1 cases v2 was written after reading the results; on the 31 new cases only the rules have run.
  • A self-hosted deployment. Only the hosted endpoints were tested.
  • A frozen v1 case set in every detail: the relative-trap notes said elder even for a relative born after the customer; the 5 held-out cases it touched were re-run with the frozen pipeline.
