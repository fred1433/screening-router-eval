Open-model routing for screening recommendations

The question: Can a smaller open-weight model prepare a defensible screening recommendation, and when does calling a larger model actually improve it, while preserving analyst sign-off?

On 80 held-out cases, each run 3 times, a small open-weight model behind shared checks did about as well as a large one: 176/180 and 178/180 answerable runs correct and supported, 60/60 and 60/60 right stops, and neither recommended excluding a true match. The router reached 180/180 with 60/60 right stops, calling the large model on 34 of 240 runs, for a resource index of 1.72 against 6.47 for the large model alone.

The checks did the protective work, not the model size. The large model's first answers made 0 such errors; after one repair call, neither model recommended excluding a true match in any run.

What I would recommend from this run: the small model behind deterministic checks as the default path, and the large model only for named, observable reasons. The router's gain over the small model alone is real but narrow here (4 runs over two cases); it earns its place by removing needless stops at a fraction of the large model's cost.

Scope: the identity disposition for a list candidate already selected upstream; adverse media, risk scoring and PEP screening are out. Customer records are synthetic. Candidate records are copied field by field from the OFAC SDN export published 2026-10-02, with the entry UID kept; nothing is alleged about anyone. Reference answers were written by the builder and checked by a separate model session, not by a compliance expert. Models: qwen/qwen3.5-9b on parasail/bf16 and qwen/qwen3.5-397b-a17b on deepinfra/fp8, through OpenRouter with fallbacks disabled.

Three case files, as the analyst would receive them

Each file holds the customer documents, the list candidate, the proposed disposition with its citations, the route taken, every check in order and the outstanding work. Nothing is approved by the system.

One thing the files do not do well enough: in the relative-trap family, where a relative of the customer is born on the listed date, the recommendation is different entity under the illustrative policy, but it names the relative in only 6 of 17 small-model runs and 1 of 17 large-model runs. The analyst needs that link in front of them; in a pilot it would be a required field.

How to read it
Counts are runs: 51 true-match, 29 distinct-entity and 20 insufficient-evidence cases, three runs each. Runs at temperature 0 are not independent, so the interval in small print is computed over cases: none of 51 true-match cases was excluded in any run, which leaves a 95% interval of 0 to 7%. Zero observed, not zero risk.
Large-model escalations
34 of 240 router runs: contradictory identifiers (15); match rests on a name spelling that is not listed (12); small output failed checks after repair (7). They corrected 6 runs (2 cases), replaced 4 small outputs that had failed their checks with valid stops, introduced no error and changed nothing material in the rest. Rules alone decided 93 runs; 11 stopped for missing evidence without a large-model call.
Before the checks
The small model's first answer failed at least one check on 62/240 model stages and the repair call fixed 57; the large model's failed on 16/240.
Not in the score
3 of the small model's right stops (the same 3 in the router) rest on a nationality agreement the file does not support: they cite the line Nationality: not captured. A stricter citation check, added after the run and replayed on every accepted output without new calls, rejects such quotes: it would have sent back 9 of 235 small-model outputs, 3 of 238 large-model outputs and 3 of 240 router outputs.

Two policies
One individual whose customer record has its passport number removed, so that the date of birth and weaker identifiers agree. Policy A (two agreeing identifiers, at least one strong) gives same entity, supported from the small model and same entity, supported from the rules; policy B (two agreeing strong identifiers) gives insufficient evidence and insufficient evidence. The policy is an input, not something the model decides.
Invented names
Every name token replaced by an invented one on 43 true-match cases. Over the answerable ones, the small model went from 26/26 to 24/26 correct and supported, the large model from 26/26 to 26/26, the router from 26/26 to 24/26. The 2 changed cases each claim a place-of-birth agreement the file does not support. This shows sensitivity to the names; it does not show whether prior knowledge of listed names helped.

The evidence behind every number

Every figure on this page is read from the result files when the page is built; the run used code commit 6901735. Prompts, routing and scoring were frozen before the final run; 20 development cases were kept apart from the 80 scored ones.

  • Cases and reference answers, and the two rounds of reference review
  • Run manifest: endpoints, settings, concurrency, retries, dates
  • Prompt, policy and routing and validators
  • Every run with its full trace and the scored summary
  • Fault suite records and the one-page memo on fine-tuning and the pilot

What this run does not show

  • Performance on real customer files. The customers are synthetic and the families of difficulty were chosen by the builder.
  • Expert agreement. The references were checked by a fresh model session that did not build them; a compliance analyst has not reviewed them.
  • A self-hosted deployment. Latency and resource use are measured on hosted endpoints on 2026-10-04; the harness takes any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, but only the hosted one was tested. OpenRouter model names are aliases the host can update.
  • The search that selects the candidate. It is assumed done upstream.
  • A frozen case set in every detail. After the run, a fresh review found that the relative-trap notes always said elder, even for a relative born after the customer; the generator was fixed and the 5 held-out cases it touched were re-run with the frozen pipeline (reruns.json).
  • Completeness of the missing-information list on a decided case. The score checks the disposition, the conflicts and the claimed agreements; an incomplete list of missing items is not penalised.

Applying the same method to one of your workflows

A first assessment of one workflow, two weeks long, counted from the day we agree the workflow and the access. It can run as a workshop with your team or inside your own environment; your code does not have to leave it.

Pilot planImplementation steps, dependencies, deployment assumptions and a schedule.

On fine-tuning, this run gives no reason to train yet. The remaining errors are of three kinds, and two belong in code: a disposition that does not follow from the model's own correct comparisons (code can apply the policy), and a field marked not captured read as a conflict (a one-line rule). Only the third, an attribute read as another (residence as nationality, nationality as place of birth), is something training might touch. The memo says which of those training could touch, what reviewed data it would need and which held-out experiment would decide it.
